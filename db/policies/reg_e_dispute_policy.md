# Regulation E — Electronic Fund Transfer Dispute Policy

**Policy OPS-410 · Retail Banking Operations Manual · Effective January 1, 2025**

## 410.1 Scope and Defined Terms

This policy governs disputes of unauthorized electronic fund transfers (EFTs) on consumer deposit accounts, as required by Regulation E (12 CFR Part 1005). "EFT" includes debit card purchases, ATM withdrawals, ACH debits, and Zelle transfers. It does not include wire transfers, which are governed by Policy OPS-520. "Business day" means Monday through Friday, excluding federal holidays. "Notice date" is the date the Bank first receives an oral or written report of the error from the consumer.

## 410.2 Investigation Window

The Bank must complete its investigation and determine whether an error occurred **within 10 business days of the notice date**. Where the Bank cannot complete the investigation within 10 business days, it may extend the investigation to **45 calendar days** from the notice date, but only if it issues provisional credit under Section 410.3. For disputes involving a point-of-sale debit card transaction, a foreign-initiated transfer, or an account open fewer than 30 days, the extended window is **90 calendar days**.

## 410.3 Provisional Credit Obligation

If the Bank extends its investigation beyond 10 business days, it **must provisionally credit the consumer's account for the full disputed amount, including any associated overdraft or NSF fees, no later than the 10th business day after the notice date**, and must notify the consumer of the credit within two business days of posting it. Provisional credit is mandatory, not discretionary. Provisional credit may only be reversed after the Bank issues a written finding of no error and provides five business days' advance notice.

## 410.4 Filing Authority — Supervisor Approval Required

**A Relationship Manager may not file a Regulation E dispute.** An RM may collect the consumer's statement, identify the disputed transactions, and prepare a draft dispute record. **The draft has no effect until a Deposit Operations Supervisor or higher reviews and approves it in writing.** The 10-business-day clock begins at the notice date, not at approval, so RMs must route drafts to a supervisor the same business day.

## 410.5 Documentation

Every dispute record retains the consumer statement, transaction identifiers, the approving supervisor's ID, and the final determination letter for **five years** from the resolution date.
